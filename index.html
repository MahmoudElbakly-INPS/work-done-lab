Key Ideas: Work Done by a Force

Learning goal: I can calculate the work done by a constant force, explain when work is positive, negative or zero, use the area under an F–x graph to find work, and connect net work to change in kinetic energy.

Core relationships

W = F d cos θ
  θ is the angle between the force and the displacement.
1 J = 1 N·m = 1 kg·m²/s²

Wnet = ΔKE = ½mvf² − ½mvi²
  (work–energy theorem)
W = area under the F–x graph
  For a spring: W = ½k(x₂² − x₁²)

Sign of work

On the horizontal bench

When F is pulled at angle θ above the horizontal: N = mg − F sin θ and kinetic friction is f = μkN. Gravity and the normal force are perpendicular to a horizontal displacement, so they do zero work. Friction opposes the motion, so it does negative work. Use g = 9.8 m/s².

Common misconceptions

MisconceptionScientific idea
"If I push hard and get tired, I did work on the object."Work on an object needs a displacement. If d = 0 (e.g., holding a box still), W = 0 J on the box.
"Work is a vector because force and displacement are vectors."Work is a scalar. A positive or negative sign shows whether energy is transferred to or from the object, not a direction.
"Every force acting on a moving object does work on it."A force perpendicular to the displacement (N and mg on a horizontal floor) does zero work.
"A larger angle always means less work."The applied force does less work at larger θ, but pulling upward reduces N and therefore reduces friction. So the net work can increase.
"For a spring, W = F × Δx."The spring force changes with x, so use the area under the F–x graph, not one value of F.
